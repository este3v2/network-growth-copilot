# Voice profile: networking outreach

Fill this in once, in about 10 minutes. `network-outreach` follows it for every draft. Ask Claude Code to interview you if you prefer: "Interview me to fill in voice/networking-outreach.md, one question at a time."

## The one rule

A message that could be sent to anyone is a failed message. Every draft must use a specific hook from that person's file: where you met, what was said, why it stuck. If the file has nothing specific, the skill says so instead of writing a template.

## Register

- Short. 2 to 5 sentences for a LinkedIn message, a few lines more for an email.
- One clear ask, not three.
- Write the way you would actually talk to someone you just met. No corporate openers or sign-offs.

## Channel differences

**LinkedIn message**
- No subject line, no formal greeting. Start with the point.
- Under about 500 characters. Shorter is better.
- Ending with a light question beats a hard request.

**Email**
- A short, specific subject line. "Following up from the Data Summit panel" beats "Great meeting you!".
- Under about 150 words for a first follow-up.
- Sign off with your first name only.

## Tone options: three drafts, you pick one

Every request returns three versions of the same message, one per tone. Same hook, same single ask. Replace these if they don't fit how you write.

| Option | Use when | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm and short** | You just met, an event or talk follow-up | 2 to 3 sentences, the `where-met` hook, one easy question |
| **Direct** | A clear business or partner ask | The point and the single ask in the first two lines, no small talk |
| **Thoughtful** | A senior contact, or re-engaging after a gap | Longer, cites something specific they said or did, the ask comes last and stays soft |

## What kills a draft

- Generic warmth ("great to connect!").
- More than one ask.
- Anything in `avoid.md`.
- Claims about them that aren't in their file.
